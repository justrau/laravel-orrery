# Known limits

- Only the `database` queue driver. For Redis, use Laravel Horizon.
- Laravel's own `queue:retry`, `queue:forget` and `queue:flush` bypass the ledger and the fence. A `queue:retry` of the same record at the same moment as an Orrery retry can publish the job twice. The package follows a native retry into the job's next cycle. After `queue:forget` or `queue:flush`, the tick removes the failed mirror rows whose bound record stays absent for max(`retry_after`, 600) seconds; a failure without a bound record ages out by `trim.failed`.
- Read-only failed-job drivers: see [Retry and forget](operations.md#retry-and-forget).
- `unknown` is a status, not a failure: a pending or reserved job whose row in `jobs` stays absent for max(`retry_after`, 600) seconds without an observed outcome (for example after `queue:clear`). When its queue connection is removed from the configuration or renamed, the tick and `orrery:reconcile` look for the job in every configured queue table: a renamed connection on the same table keeps its waiting jobs, and a job that no configured table holds becomes `unknown` with the reason `connection_removed`, which raises no stuck alert. While a table cannot be read (by the tick: a SQLite file in rollback mode), no job is marked this way; `orrery:reconcile` reads such a file. It is kept for `trim.unknown`.
- The in and out rates and the throughput counts are observations, not exact event counts: a retry of the same job is not a new arrival, and only the latest outcome of a job counts.
- A failure group is keyed by the first line of the message with its variable parts replaced: datetimes, UUIDs, ULIDs, email addresses, long hexadecimal runs, numbers of 4 or more digits, the value of a MySQL "Duplicate entry '…' for key", and the SQL after " (Connection: ". The SQLSTATE code and the driver's error number stay, so different database errors keep apart. Shorter numbers stay, so "HTTP 404" and "HTTP 503" make two groups, and so do messages with short ids ("Order 12 not found").
- `GET /orrery/api/classes` reads at most `dashboard.class_rows` per-class rows in all (the stored minutes first, then the minutes that it counts from the job rows), newest minute first. When job names change per job, some minutes of the window are not counted (`capped: true`, the minutes in `unknown_minutes`), every sum is "at least", and the listed classes are the busiest of the minutes that were read.
- The Class and Tag suggestions of the Jobs filters (`GET /orrery/api/suggestions`) read only recent values: the job names of the newest 1,000 jobs and of at most 500 classes counted in the last 60 minutes, and the monitored tags plus the tags of the newest 1,000 jobs. An older class or tag is not suggested (the answer says `limited: true`), but it can still be typed.
- Without [the optional `jobs` index](storage.md#the-optional-jobs-index), the queue measurement scans the whole `jobs` table. On SQLite no statement timeout applies, and the tick checks its deadline only between phases. So on a large table, the first tick after a big backlog can run past `orrery.tick.deadline_seconds`, and the later phases are skipped until the next tick. The three notification phases still run, each with its own share, so the alerts go on. Add the index for large queues; [the tick benchmark](performance/tick-benchmark.md) has the measurements.
- "Over limit since" comes from the minute samples of the tick: a tick more than about a minute late starts the time again.
- A bulk dispatch (`Queue::bulk()`, `Bus::batch()`) costs statements on the package connection per job inside `dispatch()`, after one read of the new `jobs` rows: two for an untagged job (the insert of its mirror row and the update that stores its job id), five for a tagged job (a transaction with the insert of its tags). Laravel inserts the jobs in one statement. So a batch of many jobs makes `dispatch()` slower by that many round trips, and the dispatching process holds each payload a few times until the call returns.
- An observation made inside a transaction of your application waits in memory for the commit. The waiting observations have a count cap and a byte budget: the smaller of 32 MB and an eighth of `memory_limit`, estimated from the payload sizes plus a small fixed size per job, so one large payload can reach it alone. A bulk push (`Queue::bulk()`, and `Bus::batch()`, which dispatches its jobs in one transaction) is one observation: when its payloads exceed the budget, the whole bulk is dropped. A dropped observation is counted in `unverified` of the health window. The first drop of each reason in a process is reported through your application's exception handler, so it reaches your log, as `deferral.count_cap` or `deferral.byte_budget`; a health window that has no other error also shows that reason as its last error. A dropped push or bulk stays invisible until a worker reserves each job, which then creates its row ([R7](architecture/lifecycle-rules.md)); reconciliation cannot repair a push.
- The package tells a running commit from one that a throwing `afterCommit` callback stopped by the call stack: a commit runs while Laravel's `DatabaseTransactionRecord::executeCallbacks()` runs its callbacks. A custom `db.transactions` manager that runs the callbacks some other way breaks this in one case: when an application callback of the commit dispatches or reserves a job of the same connection before the package's callback runs, the observations of that transaction are counted in `unverified` and not written. A worker then creates the row of such a push when it reserves the job.
- A package session has a connect timeout but no read timeout. A statement on an open connection to a server that stops answering without closing the connection (a host that goes down, a firewall that drops packets) waits until the operating system gives up on the connection. The server's statement timeout does not help there. Set TCP keepalive on the hosts, or keep the package tables on the application's own database server.
- `migrate:fresh`, `db:wipe` and Laravel's `DatabaseMigrations` trait drop the package tables without the migration's check for open operations, but only when they are on the wiped connection. With `ORRERY_CONNECTION` on another connection, the package tables and their rows stay, and the next `migrate` keeps them.
- While Orrery is off (`ORRERY_ENABLED=false`), it observes nothing. After you turn it on again, the jobs of that time have no rows or stale rows, and the per-minute charts show that time as unknown (a blind gap, see [Turning Orrery off](operations.md#turning-orrery-off)).
- Blind gaps are decided from job evidence only for a time in which no tick decided (a tick outage of more than 5 minutes, or ticks whose `sample_counts` phase failed or was skipped). In such a time, a quiet span of more than 5 minutes (no job reserved, completed or failed) shows as unknown, also when the workers were running and observing: the package cannot tell it from a time when nothing observed. An off period of 5 minutes or less, or one while the tick still ran, is not detected, and its minutes count as zero. Follow [Turning Orrery off](operations.md#turning-orrery-off): `config:cache`, then `queue:restart`, both ways. A scheduler that runs again while the workers are still off counts those minutes as zero.
- A job whose row in `jobs` left during a blind gap becomes `unknown` with the reason `blind_gap` and sends no stuck-job alert. The tick can place the loss only between two of its checks: a job that left up to 5 minutes before the last tick before the gap, or after that tick while the workers still observed (up to min(the longest max(`retry_after`, 600) of the database connections, 900) seconds, so at most 15 minutes), or after observation resumed but before the first check after the gap reached it (one tick for up to 1,000 active jobs, pending and reserved, per connection), gets this reason too. A reserved job whose reservation expired during the gap can still raise a "reservation expired" alert when the tick resumes, before the job becomes unknown.
